The Elephant in the Question

Unified models picture what they never say.

The question

On which continent does the biggest land animal
live?

The model answersAfrica

Did the model simply retrieve the answer “Africa” from its memory, or did it actually include a representation of an elephant in its computation on the way to its answer?

To investigate this, we introduce the V-lens. It visualizes a token’s hidden states at intermediate layers using the model’s own image generator. Indeed, we find an elephant represented in the hidden state at the token animal. The V-lens combines a training-free mapping of hidden states with the model’s own image generator, so no separately fitted decoder is needed.

How the V-lens works

From hidden states to a picture.

1  Read

Select a token and an extraction layer ℓ

On which continent does the biggest land animal live?

h is the extracted hidden state of the token animal at layer ℓ. Each bar is one coordinate of h, and 16 of its thousands are shown.
2  Transport

Center and rescale the hidden state

We estimate each layer’s mean and coordinate spreads from plain-text passages.

Extractedlayer ℓCenteredRescaledReady to insertlayer R
Subtract the extraction layer’s mean across the tokens of plain-text passages from each coordinate. Multiply each coordinate by its spread at layer R divided by its spread at layer ℓ. Set the vector’s length to the median distance of insertion-layer states from their mean, then add that mean.

Bands show reference spreads. Ticks mark means. Hover or tap the steps below the columns.

3  Insert

Replace the hidden state at “thing” in the image prompt

a photo of a thing

v is the centered and rescaled state. It replaces the hidden state of the token thing in the drawing prompt at the insertion layer R, and the model then draws as usual.
4  Draw

The model’s own image generator draws it

Transport details

v = μR + sR Dx‖Dx‖,   x = h − μℓ,   D = diag(σR / σℓ)

h is the extracted hidden state at layer ℓ, x the centered state and v the state inserted at layer R. μℓ and μR are the mean hidden states of the two layers, σℓ and σR the per-coordinate spreads around them. Coordinate scaling D is diagonal, and each diagonal entry is the ratio of that coordinate’s spread at layer R to its spread at layer ℓ. sR is the median distance of layer-R states from μR. All statistics come from plain-text passages unrelated to the test items, so nothing is trained or fitted on pictures (paper, Eq. 1). In the diagram above, the means, spreads and target length are illustrative.

No decoder is trained. The model draws from the replaced hidden state with its standard image-generation procedure.

Context

The same word yields different images in different contexts.

Hidden states reveal objects the question never names.

The V-lens reveals objects the model is asked to think about.

We test whether an instruction to think about an object changes the hidden states of unrelated text. We ask the model to think about a concept while it copies an unrelated sentence word by word. Then we visualize each token of the copied sentence through the V-lens at layers 24 to 26.

Successful visualizations cluster at the in Show-o2 and at the final period in HunyuanImage-3.0. Without the think instruction, the object almost never appears.

Lens tuning

After lens tuning, the V-lens shows which of two objects “it” refers to.

We train only text adapters through the V-lens, using target pictures of the object that it refers to. On unseen sentence pairs, we test whether both V-lens pictures show the correct object.
